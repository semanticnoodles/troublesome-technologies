De-identification Protocol

Project-wide

Download the protocol as PDF

This protocol applies to both research strands. It safeguards the privacy and confidentiality of participant data collected as part of the research study Troublesome Technologies and Learning Trajectories in Digital Preservation Practice. The definitions are aligned with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)1 and guidance from the Data Protection Commission (DPC).2

Definitions

Term Meaning
Personal data Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Identifiers include names, identification numbers, location data, online identifiers, or factors specific to physical, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity.
Pseudonymisation The processing of personal data in such a manner that the data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information. This additional information must be kept separately and subject to technical and organisational measures to prevent re-identification.
Anonymisation An irreversible process that alters data so that the individual is no longer identifiable under any reasonable circumstances. While not explicitly defined in the GDPR text, anonymised data falls outside the scope of data protection legislation.

De-identification procedures

To adhere to data minimisation principles, the online survey collects no personal contact details other than an email address, which participants may voluntarily provide to take part in the follow-up research stage; names are collected only from participants invited to the interview stage. Each participant is assigned a sequential Participant Identification number (PID) based on survey completion. Key pairs linking PIDs to personal identifiers (such as name and email address) will be securely stored on a password-protected UCD cloud-based storage (Microsoft OneDrive) using multi-factor authentication, with access strictly governed by the principle of least privilege, restricting access exclusively to the PI and her collaborator. Data is stored in compliance with UCD IT security policies and hosted in secure European data centres (EEA) under UCD’s institutional data protection agreement with the service provider. The key pairs file will be permanently erased within 30 days of the conclusion of Strand 2 data collection (by the end of February 2027).

Protocol

This protocol ensures the protection of personal information and sensitive institutional details in research materials while maintaining enough context to allow a proper in-depth analysis. Upon explicit request, participants will be granted the option to review the de-identified written reflection and/or interview transcript to ensure their privacy is kept.

Element Rule
Personal information Participant names are replaced with standardised alphanumeric codes (e.g., PID020).
Colleagues and personnel Non-participant individuals mentioned in text or transcripts are anonymised by omitting names and/or substituting functional roles (e.g., COLLECTIONS MANAGER), to preserve maximum fidelity to the source.
Institutions and organisations Specific names of entities (e.g., libraries, museums, universities, companies, funding bodies, projects, or consortia) are omitted or replaced with broad category placeholders such as MUSEUM, UNIVERSITY, INDUSTRIAL PARTNER, SPONSOR, or PROJECT.
Locations Specific cities or precise geographical indicators are omitted or replaced with placeholders like LOCATION, TOWN, MUNICIPALITY. Broader geographic contexts (such as countries) may be retained if they do not compromise anonymity.
Ellipses Square brackets containing ellipses ([…]) signify where specific identifying details or context have been omitted to preserve confidentiality without disrupting narrative continuity.
